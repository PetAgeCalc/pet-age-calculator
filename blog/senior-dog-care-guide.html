Signs Your Dog Is Entering Its Senior Years (And What To Do)

📅 August 2026 · ⏱ 6 min read
Home › Blog › Senior Dog Care

Dogs rarely announce that they've become seniors. The change happens gradually, and by the time it's obvious, your dog may have been quietly slowing down for months. Catching the early signs means you can adjust their care before small issues become bigger ones.

When Does "Senior" Actually Start?

It depends heavily on size. Giant breeds like Great Danes can be considered senior as early as 5-6 years old, while small breeds like Chihuahuas may not reach that stage until 10-12. Larger dogs simply age faster - both physically and in terms of typical lifespan.

Early Signs Most Owners Miss

  • Slower to rise after lying down, or hesitation before jumping onto furniture
  • Greying fur around the muzzle and eyes - often the very first visible sign
  • Reduced enthusiasm for walks or play, without obvious pain
  • Cloudier eyes, a normal age-related change distinct from cataracts
  • More naps, and lighter sleep at night
  • Slight weight change in either direction as metabolism shifts

Practical Changes That Help

1. Adjust the Diet

Senior dog foods are formulated with fewer calories to match a slower metabolism, along with added joint-support ingredients like glucosamine. Switching too early can lead to unnecessary weight loss, so time it around when your dog's activity level genuinely starts dropping.

2. Increase Vet Visit Frequency

Twice-yearly checkups, instead of once, catch problems like early kidney changes or dental disease while they're still manageable and inexpensive to treat.

3. Support Joints Proactively

Non-slip mats on hard floors, ramps instead of stairs where possible, and consistent light exercise all help preserve mobility for longer.

4. Watch for Cognitive Changes

Confusion, disrupted sleep patterns, or seeming "lost" in familiar places can be early signs of canine cognitive dysfunction - worth mentioning to your vet rather than dismissing as "just old age."

The Bottom Line

Senior dogs don't need less care - they need different care. Spotting the early signs and adjusting food, activity, and vet visits accordingly is one of the simplest ways to keep the senior years comfortable rather than difficult.
